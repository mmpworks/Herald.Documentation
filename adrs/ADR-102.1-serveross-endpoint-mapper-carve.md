# ADR-102.1 — ServerOSS endpoint mapper carve: clean 8-endpoint reimplementation

- **Status:** Proposed
- **Date:** 2026-05-18
- **Stage:** 2 (sub-ADR under ADR-102)
- **Owner:** Richard (Glenn flagged this as architecture-judgment, not mechanical)
- **Mirrors:** umbrella `herald`

## Context

The current `HeraldEndpointMapper.cs` registers about 90 endpoints
across `MapManagementEndpoints`, `MapLiveLogEndpoints`,
`MapLogFileEndpoints`, `MapIngestEndpoint`, `MapOtlpIngestEndpoint`,
`MapOtlpProtobufIngestEndpoint`, `MapOtlpGrpcIngestEndpoint`, and
`MapHealthEndpoint`. The file is 2,530 lines, organised around
scoped route groups (read / write / admin) that assume an auth
middleware sits in front. ServerOSS publishes eight endpoints (seven
business plus `/health`) and has no auth at all.

Glenn flagged this as a per-line keep/delete decision that needs an
architectural judgment, not a mechanical sweep. Two paths exist:
strip down the existing mapper, or reimplement clean against
`MMP.Herald.RestApi.Host` interfaces. The choice has follow-on cost
for every subsequent change to either ServerOSS or the paid mappers.

## Decision

### Reimplement clean, do not strip

`Herald.ServerOSS.EndpointMapper` is a **clean reimplementation against
`MMP.Herald.RestApi.Host` interfaces**, not a stripped copy of the
2,530-line file. The scoped-group pattern (read / write / admin route
groups) does **not** survive into ServerOSS — there is no auth, so
no scope split. ServerOSS uses one ungated route group.

The clean reimplementation is roughly 150 LoC against the current
mapper's 2,530.

### Endpoints carried over (with their current handler sources)

- **`GET /api/pipelines`** — current `read.MapGet("/api/registry", ...)`
  at line 957. Keep handler logic, rename path.
- **`GET /api/pipelines/{name}`** — current
  `read.MapGet("/api/pipeline/{name}/state", ...)` at line 332 plus
  `read.MapGet("/api/pipeline/{name}/components", ...)` at line 359.
  **Merge into one handler** that returns `{ state, components }`.
- **`PUT /api/pipelines/{name}`** — combined
  `write.MapPost("/api/registry/{name}/propose", ...)` at line 1058
  plus `write.MapPost("/api/registry/{name}/commit", ...)` at line
  1168. **Merge into a single transactional PUT** (propose-plus-commit
  in one call). Transactions stay internal in ServerOSS — there is no
  multi-step admin UI to surface them.
- **`POST /api/sinks`** — currently
  `admin.MapPut("/api/sinkProviders/{kind}/config", ...)` at line 874.
  Keep handler; change verb to POST and path to `/api/sinks` with
  sink kind in the body.
- **`GET /api/sinks`** — current
  `read.MapGet("/api/sinkProviders", ...)` at line 756. Keep verbatim,
  rename path.
- **`GET /api/system/license`** — new handler, returns
  `{ edition: HeraldVersion.CurrentEdition.ToString(), metaItems: [] }`.
- **`GET /api/system/capabilities`** — new handler, returns `[]`.
- **`GET /health`** — current `app.MapGet("/health", ...)` at line
  2300. Keep verbatim.

## Alternatives considered

### Strip-down path

Keep the existing 2,530-line mapper structure, delete the handlers
ServerOSS doesn't ship, and live with the scoped-group and
auth-coupled abstractions in place. **Rejected.** The residual coupling
makes the resulting file harder to read than a clean 8-endpoint file.
The scoped-group convention exists to express auth boundaries;
ServerOSS has no auth, so the convention adds noise without benefit.

### Generated mapper from a contract spec

Generate the ServerOSS endpoint mapper from an OpenAPI spec or from
the contracts package itself. **Rejected for Stage 2.** The tooling
cost is not justified at eight endpoints. If ServerOSS grows beyond
a tight handful of endpoints, revisit then.

## Consequences

**Positive.** The ServerOSS endpoint mapper is small enough to read
in one sitting and audit in another. The scoped-group convention
stays in the paid mappers where it earns its keep. The clean
reimplementation lets ServerOSS evolve its endpoint shapes (path,
verb, body) without inheriting renames the paid binaries don't want.

**Negative.** The carve is not mechanical for Glenn — Richard owns
the per-handler decisions for any future ServerOSS additions.
Documented in this ADR so the discipline survives a handoff.

The Echo race fixture (R20) tests `PUT /api/pipelines/{name}` against
concurrent `POST /api/sinks` on the new ServerOSS mapper specifically.
The fixture lives in the ServerOSS test project before Stage 2 close.

## Quick picture

> Picture renovating a house. You could keep every wall and shim around
> what you want to remove — but the load-bearing wall in the middle of
> what should be the new open kitchen still tells you where the old
> rooms were. Or you could tear it down to studs and rebuild the floor
> plan you actually want. ServerOSS's endpoint shape is different
> enough from the paid mappers that the second option is cheaper to
> live with.

## Rationale tied to CUPID + DRY

This decision deliberately accepts a small DRY violation: ServerOSS
will have its own implementation of endpoint handlers that resemble
the paid binaries' handlers. That's the trade ADR-102 names —
duplication is the price of the IP wall. The duplication budget is
bounded (~150 LoC) and the quarterly drift audit watches it.

The decision supports CUPID *Predictable*: a reader of the ServerOSS
mapper sees exactly the endpoints ServerOSS publishes, in exactly the
shape ServerOSS publishes them. No conditional branches based on auth
state, no opt-in scopes, no commented-out handlers. The smaller surface
is the easier surface to reason about.
