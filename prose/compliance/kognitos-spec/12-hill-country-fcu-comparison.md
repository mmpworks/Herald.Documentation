## New comparison points specific to this chapter

### A. Single-substrate single-organization §10.21 cross-vendor handover as canonical reference shape

**The audit-room question.** *"The handover was Hill Country → Hill Country — one institution replacing one vendor with another, inside one AWS substrate, with one HSM root, under contractual cooperation between the two vendors. The byte-equal hash join took nine minutes. The chain held the anchor. Where, in Kognitos, do we record the structural shape of the handover — vendor-replacement-within-substrate vs cross-organizational-partnership?"*

**TesseraSeal.** §10.21 carries `handover_kind` as a row attribute with at least two values: `vendor_replacement_within_substrate` (Hill Country today) and `cross_organizational_partnership` (Eberhardt × Lumière at Ch11). The taxonomy lets readers distinguish the two structural shapes at the chain-row level. Both forms compose with §10.17 dual-signature pair and produce byte-equal hash join across the handover boundary.

**Kognitos.** Field 12 (tamper-evident integrity proof) reads "a proof exists" against the chain. The row shape does not admit a distinction between vendor-replacement-within-substrate and cross-organizational-partnership; the same Field 12 confirmation reads identically against both Hill Country's chain and Eberhardt × Lumière's chain.

**Inarticulability gap.** The framework cannot articulate the structural taxonomy of the handover. Field 8 (reasoning/rationale) could carry the handover-kind as free-text annotation; the taxonomy as a row-attribute discriminator would be lost.

**Structural reason for the gap.** Kognitos's row architecture assumes a single common shape for any chain proof; it does not admit attributes that distinguish structural classes of the same proof type. The reference spec models §10.21 as a family of related but structurally-different handovers; the framework cannot carry families.

**Honest assessment.** Severity: medium. The gap is real but the consequences in confirmation-posture engagements are low — the chain reads clean either way; the taxonomy matters most when comparing engagements across chapters (Hill Country vs Eberhardt × Lumière) rather than within a single chapter's deliverable. Future cross-substrate engagements may sharpen this gap.

### B. ECOA marketing-event-to-credit-decision parent-linkage chain pivot across vendor handover

**The audit-room question.** *"The member received a $35K auto-loan marketing offer at 6.250% under the legacy Total Expert ML scoring weights in February. The member applied for the loan three weeks later under the new in-house ML scoring weights. The credit decision approved at 6.625% on slightly different terms. The credit-decision chain row references the prior-offer chain row by `prior_offer_run_id` + `prior_offer_seq` under MAC. The ECOA defense rests on the cryptographic parent-binding. Where, in Kognitos, do we record the binding?"*

**TesseraSeal.** §10.11.1 `audit.ecoa.adverse_action.*` family carries `prior_offer_run_id` + `prior_offer_seq` as MAC-bound parent-linkage fields on the credit-decision chain entry. The linkage is cryptographic — the credit-decision row's MAC includes the prior-offer chain row's ID and sequence number; verifier walks the linkage by chain ID resolution. The binding spans the vendor handover because the marketing scoring side's chain (legacy Total Expert era) and the credit-decision side's chain (new vendor era) share the same `hillcountry` tenant and one HSM root; the parent-linkage references resolve across the vendor handover via the §10.21 anchor.

**Kognitos.** Field 8 (reasoning/rationale) could carry the linkage as free-text annotation: "this credit decision references the prior marketing offer from chain row hillcountry/marketing-scoring/2026-02-04#3127." Field 11 (approval/oversight) names the credit decision's approval surface but does not bind the prior-offer chain row. No field carries the cryptographic parent-binding.

**The under-reporting.** Under ECOA disparate-impact litigation, the defense rests on demonstrating that the credit decision's rate-and-term differed from the prior offer for non-discriminatory reasons, with full traceability to the scoring weights at both moments. The reference spec produces the traceability as a cryptographic chain pivot. Kognitos produces it as free-text editorial.

**Speculation gap.** Under Kognitos, the auditor would have to speculate the chain pivot into Field 8 annotation, with the cryptographic binding under MAC unrepresented. Under regulator examination — NCUA AIRES Module 6 fair-lending review or CFPB §1033 follow-on disclosure — the speculation does not match what the regulator expects to see.

**Honest assessment.** Severity: high for any institution with ECOA / fair-lending audit posture where AI-scored marketing offers feed AI-scored credit decisions, particularly across vendor-handover boundaries where the scoring weights changed between the prior offer and the decision. Pattern recurs across consumer-credit institutions (banks, credit unions, fintech lenders) operating any marketing-to-credit-decision automation.

### C. §10.69 per-customer disclosure subtree spanning §10.21 cross-vendor anchor leaf

**TesseraSeal.** §10.69 + §10.21 + §4.2 compose to produce "one §1033 disclosure returns one unified per-member audit trail across both vendor eras" as a structural property. The §10.69 per-customer disclosure subtree spans the §10.21 cross-vendor anchor leaf cleanly because the anchor binds the legacy-vendor era's chain entries and the new-vendor era's chain entries under one tenant-day Merkle seal. **Honest assessment.** Severity: high for any institution with CFPB §1033 obligations spanning vendor-handover boundaries. Pattern recurs across consumer-finance institutions (banks, credit unions, fintech, BNPL providers) that have undertaken or will undertake AI-vendor-handovers.

### D. §10.66 model-weight lineage DAG resolution across vendor-replacement handover

**The audit-room question.** *"The current Hill Country marketing-scoring model resolves back through three retrain ancestors to the original 2024 Total Expert launch model in under three minutes. The DAG is parent-model-linked with retrain-event timestamps and training-data-manifest hashes. Where, in Kognitos, do we record the lineage DAG?"*

**TesseraSeal.** §10.66 carries the model-weight lineage DAG as a row-attribute structure: each retrain event is a chain row with `parent_model_id` + `parent_model_version` + `retrain_event_timestamp` + `training_data_manifest_sha256` + `audit.model_handover.*` cross-references. **Kognitos.** Field 4 (tools/models used) names the current deployed model. Nothing in the row carries parent-model lineage. The DAG would have to be argued out as editorial annotation under Field 8 (reasoning) or as a separate compliance document outside the chain entirely.

**The under-reporting.** Under MRM (model risk management) review — SR 11-7 substrate, or any institutional MRM committee — the lineage is the load-bearing artifact for understanding model evolution and risk-attribution. The reference spec produces the lineage as a chain-bound structure; Kognitos produces it as out-of-chain documentation.

**Honest assessment.** Severity: medium for any institution with mature MRM discipline. Pattern recurs across consumer-finance institutions, healthcare ML deployments, and any AI-deployment surface with multiple retrain cycles.
